# Cardiovascular Gene Therapy Clinical Endpoints

A research resource for understanding clinical endpoint selection, efficacy assessment, safety monitoring, and interpretation of clinical evidence in investigational gene therapies for inherited cardiovascular diseases.

## Overview

Clinical endpoints help define what a study is designed to measure and how investigators evaluate whether an investigational therapy may provide meaningful benefit. In inherited cardiovascular diseases, endpoint selection requires consideration of disease biology, cardiac function, clinical events, biomarkers, patient characteristics, and the limitations of available evidence.

This repository examines the principles behind cardiovascular gene therapy clinical assessment. It supports structured research into how clinical studies are designed, how outcomes are measured, and how published results and clinical development updates should be interpreted.

The resource covers general clinical research principles and disease-specific considerations across inherited cardiovascular conditions. It does not replace clinical trial protocols, regulatory guidance, or professional medical judgment.

## Research Areas

### 1. Clinical Endpoint Selection

- Primary, secondary, and exploratory endpoints
- Clinical outcomes versus surrogate endpoints
- Endpoint selection in rare cardiovascular diseases
- Alignment between study objectives and outcome measures
- Clinical relevance, measurement reliability, and interpretability

### 2. Cardiac Function and Disease Assessment

- Cardiac imaging and ventricular function measures
- Structural and functional indicators of disease progression
- Arrhythmia-related outcomes and cardiac event assessment
- Functional capacity and patient-relevant outcomes
- Considerations for longitudinal assessment in inherited cardiomyopathies

### 3. Biomarkers and Evidence Interpretation

- Biomarker selection and intended use
- Relationships between biomarkers and clinical outcomes
- Changes from baseline and longitudinal trends
- Distinguishing biological activity from demonstrated clinical benefit
- Limitations of surrogate measures in small clinical studies

### 4. Safety Monitoring

- Adverse events and serious adverse events
- Cardiac safety assessment
- Immune responses and other potential gene therapy-related risks
- Laboratory and biomarker monitoring
- Duration of follow-up and assessment of delayed safety concerns

Safety considerations vary by investigational product, vector, transgene, administration approach, and study protocol. This repository does not assume that all gene therapy programs share the same risk profile.

### 5. Clinical Study Design

- Study objectives and endpoint hierarchies
- Baseline characteristics and eligibility criteria
- Sample size considerations in rare diseases
- Follow-up periods and assessment schedules
- Missing data, variability, and interpretation of small patient populations
- Sources of uncertainty in early-phase and pivotal studies

### 6. Durability and Long-Term Follow-Up

- Persistence of observed clinical effects
- Longitudinal cardiac function and clinical outcomes
- Continued safety surveillance
- Distinguishing short-term responses from durable benefit
- Limitations of follow-up duration and available patient data

### 7. Disease-Specific Clinical Context

Clinical endpoint interpretation depends on the underlying disease mechanism and the intended therapeutic effect.

This repository provides a framework for examining clinical assessment in:

- **Danon disease:** Relevant cardiac manifestations and outcome measures in the context of LAMP2-associated disease.
- **PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy:** Arrhythmia-related outcomes, structural disease progression, and cardiac function.
- **BAG3-associated dilated cardiomyopathy:** Ventricular function, heart failure-related outcomes, and disease progression.

These topics are research areas, not assertions that a single endpoint is appropriate for every program or patient population.

## Applying the Framework to Investigational Programs

The repository may be used to organize publicly available clinical information about investigational cardiovascular gene therapies, including Rocket Pharmaceuticals programs such as RP-A501, RP-A601, and RP-A701.

For each program, research should distinguish between:

1. The stated objectives and endpoints in the relevant trial documentation.
2. The measurements and follow-up periods specified by the study protocol or public trial record.
3. Results actually reported by the sponsor or in scientific publications.
4. The sponsor's interpretation of those results.
5. Independent interpretation, including limitations and unanswered questions.

Program-specific endpoints, trial status, enrollment, dosing, and reported outcomes must be verified against current primary sources. They should not be inferred from the disease mechanism or from another investigational program.

## Evidence and Source Standards

The quality of clinical interpretation depends on the quality and context of the underlying evidence.

Research should prioritize:

- Clinical trial records and study documentation
- Peer-reviewed scientific publications
- Conference presentations with identifiable source details
- Regulatory documents and published guidance
- Sponsor disclosures, clearly identified as sponsor-reported information
- Established scientific literature on inherited cardiovascular disease and clinical outcome assessment

When documenting a claim, include the source title, publication or update date when available, relevant URL, and the specific finding being discussed.

Clearly distinguish published findings from hypotheses, planned analyses, preliminary observations, and editorial interpretation. Where evidence is incomplete or conflicting, document the uncertainty rather than presenting a definitive conclusion.
